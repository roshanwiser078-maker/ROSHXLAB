NUTRITION · FUNDAMENTALS

Build A Balanced Meal.

A simple way to combine protein, carbohydrates, vegetables or fruit, healthy fats and fluids into a meal that supports everyday energy and activity.

THE BASIC IDEA

A balanced meal doesn't need to be complicated.

Good nutrition is less about creating a perfect plate and more about eating a variety of foods regularly.

A useful meal usually combines several food groups so your body gets different nutrients and enough energy to support school, training, daily activities and normal growth.

BUILD YOUR MEAL

Think in food groups.

01 · PROTEIN

Protein Foods

Protein foods can include eggs, dal, beans, chickpeas, paneer, soy foods, fish, chicken and other protein-rich foods.

02 · CARBOHYDRATES

Energy Foods

Rice, roti, oats, potatoes, fruits and other carbohydrate-rich foods can provide energy for school, exercise and daily life.

03 · PLANTS

Vegetables & Fruit

Add vegetables or fruit for vitamins, minerals, fiber and variety. Different colors can help you eat a wider range of foods.

04 · FATS & FLUIDS

Healthy Fats + Water

Nuts, seeds and other nutritious fat sources can fit into meals, while water helps support normal hydration.

SIMPLE EXAMPLES

What can a balanced meal look like?

Example 01 · Indian meal

Dal + rice or roti + mixed vegetables + fruit + water.

Example 02 · Simple workout meal

Eggs or paneer + roti + vegetables + fruit + water.

Example 03 · Vegetarian option

Chickpeas or dal + rice/roti + vegetables + nuts or seeds + water.

PROTEIN

Don't forget the protein source.

Protein is one important part of a balanced diet. It supports normal growth and helps your body maintain and repair tissues.

You don't need to rely on supplements to include protein. Everyday foods such as eggs, milk, paneer, dal, beans, chickpeas, soy foods, nuts, seeds, fish and chicken can all contribute.

CARBOHYDRATES

Carbohydrates are not the enemy.

Carbohydrates are an important source of energy. They can be especially useful around active days and workouts.

Rice, roti, oats, potatoes, fruits and other carbohydrate-containing foods can all fit into a balanced eating pattern.

VARIETY

Change your foods regularly.

You don't have to eat the exact same meal every day. Different foods provide different nutrients, so variety makes your overall diet more flexible.

Try changing your vegetables, fruits, protein sources and carbohydrate sources throughout the week.

TRAINING & FOOD

Your meals should support your activity.

If you train regularly, your body needs enough energy and nutrients to support activity as well as normal growth and recovery.

A balanced meal can be useful before or after training, depending on your schedule and appetite. There is no need to make nutrition unnecessarily complicated.

HYDRATION

Keep water part of the routine.

Water is a simple everyday drink choice. Keep water available during school, workouts and other daily activities.

One important reminder

A balanced meal is not about eating as little as possible or following strict food rules. Especially during the teenage years, your body is still growing and needs adequate nutrition.

Focus on variety, regular meals, nutritious foods and enough energy for your daily life.

ROSHXLAB NOTE

Keep nutrition practical.

You don't need a perfect meal every time. Build better habits by making nutritious choices more often and learning how different foods fit into your routine.

If you have specific nutrition concerns, medical conditions, allergies or questions about your individual needs, talk with a parent or guardian and a qualified healthcare professional.
