BETTER SLEEP HABITS

Simple Things That Help.

You do not need a complicated recovery routine. Start with habits you can actually repeat.

01

Consistent Bedtime

Try to keep your sleep and wake times reasonably consistent.

02

Wind Down

Give yourself some quiet time before bed instead of immediately jumping from a busy activity into sleep.

03

Comfortable Environment

A dark, quiet and comfortable sleeping environment can make it easier to rest.

04

Manage Screen Time

Reducing stimulating screen use before bed can make winding down easier. Keeping screens away for about an hour before bed can also support a healthier bedtime routine.

02 Â· RECOVERY

Recovery Is More Than Sleep.

Good recovery also comes from sensible training, regular meals, hydration and taking breaks when needed.

TRAINING

Do Not Train Hard Every Day

Your training plan should include easier periods and rest so you can keep progressing.

NUTRITION

Eat Regularly

Balanced meals provide the nutrients and energy your body needs throughout the day.

HYDRATION

Stay Hydrated

Drinking water regularly is a simple part of supporting normal daily activity.

LISTEN TO YOUR BODY

When To Ease Off.

Recovery needs can change. Pay attention to how you feel instead of forcing every workout.

01

Unusual Fatigue

Feeling unusually tired can be a reason to reduce training intensity and prioritize rest.

02

Performance Dropping

If your normal training suddenly feels much harder, recovery may need more attention.

03

Persistent Pain

Do not push through ongoing or significant pain. Tell a parent, guardian or qualified professional when appropriate.

IMPORTANT

More Training Isn't Always Better.

Progress is not about doing the maximum amount of exercise every day.

A sustainable routine balances training, food, sleep, rest and everyday life.
