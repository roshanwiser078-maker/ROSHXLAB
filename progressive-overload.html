THE FUNDAMENTAL

Don't just
repeat. Improve.

Your body adapts to the training you give it. Once an exercise becomes easier, repeating the exact same workload forever may provide less of a challenge.

Progressive overload means gradually increasing the challenge of your training while keeping good technique and allowing enough recovery.

01 Â· UNDERSTAND IT

What Is Progressive Overload?

It is a gradual approach to making your training more challenging as you become more capable.

01

More Reps

If an exercise feels manageable with your current reps, you can gradually work toward more repetitions while maintaining good form.

02

More Resistance

With suitable equipment, a gradual increase in resistance can make an exercise more challenging.

03

Better Technique

Improving control, range of motion and technique can make an exercise more effective without simply adding more weight.

04

More Control

Slower, controlled repetitions can increase the challenge while helping you focus on movement quality.

02 Â· APPLY IT

How To Use It.

Progress does not need to be complicated. Start with something manageable and build gradually.

01

Master The Movement

Learn the exercise and focus on controlled, comfortable technique before trying to make it harder.

02

Build Consistency

Practice your routine consistently so you can understand how your body responds to training.

03

Increase Gradually

When the current workload becomes manageable, make one small change rather than changing everything at once.

04

Recover

Give your body enough time to recover between challenging sessions. Progress happens over time, not from rushing every workout.

03 Â· SIMPLE EXAMPLE

One Exercise.
Small Progress.

Imagine you are comfortable performing a bodyweight squat with good technique.

Instead of suddenly making the exercise extremely difficult, you could gradually increase repetitions or improve control while keeping the movement comfortable and technically sound.

Once that becomes manageable, you can introduce an appropriate amount of resistance if you have suitable equipment and supervision where needed.

04 Â· IMPORTANT

More Isn't Always Better.

Progressive overload should be gradual. Trying to make every workout harder can interfere with technique, recovery and consistency.

01

Technique First

Good movement quality comes before adding difficulty.

02

Progress Slowly

Small improvements repeated over time can add up.

03

Respect Recovery

Rest, sleep and regular meals are part of training.

05 Â· AVOID THESE

Common Mistakes.

01

Adding Too Much Too Soon

Progress should be gradual rather than a race to use more resistance.

02

Ignoring Technique

A heavier or harder movement is not useful if your technique breaks down.

03

Skipping Recovery

Training is only one part of improvement. Recovery matters too.

04

Changing Everything

Making many changes at once can make it difficult to understand what is actually helping.
