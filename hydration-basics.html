NUTRITION · HYDRATION

Hydration Basics.

Understand why fluids matter, how to make hydration part of your daily routine and what changes when you're active or exercising.

WHY IT MATTERS

Water is part of everyday fitness.

Your body needs water for many normal functions, including circulation, temperature regulation and other everyday processes.

Hydration is especially worth paying attention to when you're exercising, sweating or spending time in hot and humid conditions.

Hydration works alongside the rest of your routine. Your overall nutrition, balanced meals and recovery habits all contribute to a healthy approach to training.

THE BASICS

Keep hydration simple.

01 · EVERYDAY

Drink Throughout The Day

Don't wait until the end of the day to think about fluids. Make water part of your normal routine.

02 · ACTIVITY

Drink Around Exercise

Water is useful before, during and after physical activity, especially when you're sweating.

03 · WEATHER

Pay Attention To Heat

Hot and humid conditions can increase fluid needs, particularly when you're physically active.

04 · ROUTINE

Carry Water

Keeping a water bottle nearby can make drinking regularly much easier.

HYDRATION & TRAINING

When you work out.

For normal workouts, water is a simple choice for staying hydrated. Make sure you have fluids available and drink during your activity.

Longer or very intense exercise, especially in hot conditions, can require more attention to fluids and electrolytes. Individual needs can vary depending on the activity, environment and amount of sweating.

Simple rule

Don't turn hydration into a complicated numbers game. Pay attention to thirst, activity, weather and how you're feeling.

NOTICE THE SIGNS

Your body can give you clues.

Thirst, dry mouth, headache, lightheadedness, cramps and darker or less frequent urine can be signs that someone may be becoming dehydrated.

These signs are not a reason to panic, but they are a reminder to pay attention to fluids and your environment.

DRINK CHOICES

Water first.

Plain water is a practical everyday drink choice. Milk can also contribute fluids and nutrients.

Sugary drinks are best kept limited. Energy drinks are not recommended for children and adolescents, particularly because they can contain caffeine and large amounts of added sugar.

EASY HABITS

Make hydration automatic.

HABIT 01

Start Your Day

Keep water available when you start your morning.

HABIT 02

Carry A Bottle

Keep a reusable bottle nearby at school, home or during training.

HABIT 03

Drink With Meals

Having water with meals makes hydration part of an existing routine.

HABIT 04

Plan For Training

Bring water with you instead of waiting until you're already thirsty.

BUILD THE BIGGER PICTURE

Hydration is only one part of nutrition.

Staying hydrated is easier when the rest of your eating routine is sensible too. Learn more about balanced meals and how protein fits into a balanced diet .

If your goal is building strength and muscle, hydration should support your overall training routine rather than replace good food, adequate recovery or consistent exercise.

You can also read our protein for muscle-building guide for more practical nutrition information.

Important for teens

During the teenage years, your body is still growing. Nutrition and hydration should support normal growth, school, daily life and physical activity—not restrictive goals or extreme routines.

If you have repeated dizziness, severe symptoms, ongoing dehydration concerns or another health issue, speak with a parent or guardian and a qualified healthcare professional.

ROSHXLAB NOTE

Keep it simple. Stay consistent.

You don't need a complicated hydration system. Keep water nearby, drink regularly and pay more attention to hydration when you're active or exposed to heat.
